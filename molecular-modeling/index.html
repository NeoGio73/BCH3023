1Your unknown protein

The protein in the viewer is a real structure, worked out by X-ray crystallography. Its name, where it came from and who solved it have been taken out of its file. By the end of this lab you will know what it is, and you will have found it from its structure and its sequence alone.

Turn it by dragging, zoom with the scroll wheel or by pinching, and point at an atom (or tap it) to see its name below the viewer. Hydrogens are not shown: X-ray crystallography rarely finds them.

2Views and axes

Tick Show the axes. For each view, which end of which axis comes out of the screen toward you?

3The two ends of the chain

A protein chain has two ends. At the N-terminus, the first residue's nitrogen is free (nitrogens are blue). At the C-terminus, the last residue's carboxyl group is free (oxygens are red). Both ends are bonded to an α-carbon.

4The sequence

The file itself is below the questions. Its SEQRES lines give the whole sequence in three-letter codes, from the N-terminus to the C-terminus.

Open the file ()

Each line starts with what kind of record it is: SEQRES the sequence, HELIX and SHEET the helices and strands, SSBOND the disulfide bonds, ATOM one atom of the chain and where it is (x, y, z in ångströms), HETATM an atom of anything else in the crystal.


        

      


      

        
5Helices, strands and disulfides

        
Draw the protein as a Cartoon: helices show as coils and β-strands as flat arrows. The file's HELIX, SHEET and SSBOND lines say where each one is.

        

        

      


      

        
6Side chains

        
The Show buttons draw some side chains large. Acidic: aspartate and glutamate. Basic: arginine, histidine and lysine. Aromatic: phenylalanine, tyrosine and tryptophan. Count them from the viewer, the sequence or the file.

        

      


      

        
7An angle at an α-carbon

        
Measure the N–Cα–C angle of a residue: press Measure: Angle, then click its N, its α-carbon (CA) and its C, in that order.

        

        

        
      


      

        
8Anything else in the file?

        
Press Show: Other molecules, and look for HETATM lines in the file. Many crystals hold metal ions, a heme or other groups the protein needs, and some hold ions or solvents from the crystallization (sulfate SO4, glycerol GOL, chloride CL and others).

        
      


      

        
9What protein is this?

        
Search your sequence with BLAST (protein BLAST, blastp). The top hit with close to 100% identity over the whole sequence names your protein and the organism it came from. Several organisms may share exactly the same sequence; say so if they do.
