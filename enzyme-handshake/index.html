The Enzyme's Handshake

Two sessions in which enzymes do organic chemistry and are judged by how well they tell mirror-image molecules apart: baker’s yeast reducing a ketone (this page), then a lipase sorting the two enantiomers of an alcohol (Part 2). Read the prelab review first, and keep the guide to the lab (PDF) open beside it. Each step can be done in the room, with a click on the thing it uses, or with its button below.

1The room

The NMR room of the Exam 1 Unknown lab, with its spectrometer, IR and GC-MS, and new things along the wall to its left: a fume hood for the solvents, the extraction and the column, and a wet bench for the yeast, the filtration, the rotary evaporator and the polarimeter.

Setting up the room

What each place is for

  • The yeast (Session 1): baker’s yeast is woken in warm sugar water in the water bath, then ferments with ethyl acetoacetate in the flask on the stirrer, open to the air, over days on a sped-up clock.
  • Filtration: the broth is filtered through a bed of Celite in the Büchner funnel, by suction from the pump, with a trap between them; the balance weighs the products.
  • Rotary evaporator: takes the solvent off each product.
  • Polarimeter: a digital one, beside it. It measures the angle through which the product turns polarized light, from which you work out how pure the (S) enantiomer is.
  • Fume hood: the separatory funnel (the extraction), the solvents, the TLC jar and the flash column that separates the ester from the alcohol in Session 2. The shaker incubator on the wet bench runs the lipase’s reaction at 40 °C.
  • NMR, IR and GC-MS: as in the Exam 1 Unknown lab; the GC will carry a chiral column, which separates the two enantiomers.

The 1H NMR of your product

2Part 2: the lipase
