The Hemoglobin Clinic

Patient  

Session 1 of 2. A patient's blood sample has come to the clinic's laboratory. Today you amplify the part of the patient's β-globin gene (HBB) where the common hemoglobin variants lie, with primers you design yourself, alongside the clinic's control DNA and a tube with no DNA at all, and you set up a DdeI digest of the products and a tube for sequencing for next time. Before you start, read the prelab review; the guide (a PDF) takes you through every step at the bench. Go to Session 2

Session 2 of 2. Your PCR products and their digests are on the bench from Session 1. Today you cast an agarose gel, run them on it beside a ladder, photograph it on the blue-light box and read the size of every band; then you read the sequencing trace, work from the gene to the protein, and make your diagnosis. The prelab review explains each method, and the guide (a PDF) every step. Back to Session 1

1Which gene?

The laboratory has read 500 bases of DNA from the patient's sample. Find out which gene it comes from: copy the read, paste it into NCBI's nucleotide BLAST (blastn, database "Nucleotide collection (nr/nt)" or "RefSeq Select"), run it, and read the top hit.

1Your primers

    
Design two primers that amplify HBB from before its first codon to past codon 42, where every variant the clinic tests for lies. Use NCBI's Primer-BLAST on the gene's reference record, NG_059281.1, and check each pair here: the page tests it against the rules below and the reference sequence. Write each primer 5′ to 3′. The bench makes a tube of each at 10 µM.

    3The digest
    
    
    DdeI cuts DNA wherever it reads C^TNAG (N is any base). The sickle-cell mutation (HbS, codon 6 GAG to GTG) changes one such site. Each digest holds 20 µL: 10 µL of PCR product, the digest buffer at 1x (from 10x), 10 units of DdeI (from 10 units/µL), and water. It goes an hour into the heat block at 37 °C.

    5For the sequencing core
    
    
    The core reads one strand of the patient's product by Sanger sequencing, from one primer. First the leftover primers and dNTPs of the PCR must go: the clean-up enzymes (an exonuclease that eats single-stranded DNA and a phosphatase) work for 15 minutes at 37 °C and are killed by 15 minutes at 80 °C. Only then does the sequencing primer go in. The tube holds 10 µL.

    5The sequence
    
    
    The core has read your tubes. Each trace shows four colours of peak, one for each base (A green, C blue, G black, T red), from the base after your sequencing primer; the letters above are the bases called, with an IUPAC letter (R, Y, W, S, K, M) where two peaks stand together. Where the patient carries two different alleles, the trace shows two peaks at the same place. Where both alleles carry the same change, there is no double peak at all: only comparing the bases called with the reference sequence finds it (NCBI's "Align two or more sequences" in BLAST does this, against NG_059281.1).
    
    
     
    
    
    
  
    

  
    
    
    6From gene to protein
    
    
    HBB's message is made from three exons; the introns between them begin with GT and end with AG and are spliced out. Lehninger numbers the β chain from its first amino acid, valine, after the starting methionine is removed.
    
    
    
      
      
      
    
    
    
    
    
      
      
    
    
    
    
      
    
      
    
        
        
        Residue 6 of each β chain is drawn as sticks.
      
    
    
    
    
    In deoxygenated HbS, the valine at position 6 of each β chain is a sticky, hydrophobic patch on the surface. It fits a pocket (Phe85 and Leu88) on a β chain of another deoxy-HbS molecule, so the molecules join into long fibers that bend red cells into sickles.
    
  
    

  
    
    
    7Your diagnosis
    
    
    Put the evidence together: which allele of HBB is on each of the patient's chromosomes, and what that means for the patient. Look up each change you found in ClinVar or the HbVar database. The page checks only that your answer agrees with your own gel and trace; your instructor marks the diagnosis.
