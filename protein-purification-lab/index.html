Purifying a Glowing Protein

Unknown  

Part 1 of 2. Your bacteria have been made to produce a fluorescent protein with a His-tag. Today you break the cells open, clear the lysate in the centrifuge and purify the protein on a nickel column. Keep the samples the bench asks for: Part 2 measures them.

1At the bench

Work in the room itself (click a bottle to take a pipetful, then click where it goes; click a tube to pick it up, then click where it goes) or with the buttons beside it. If a step is about to go wrong, the bench warns you first; if something does go wrong, it tells you what it did and lets you start that part again.

Setting up the bench

Bench clock 0:00
µL (10 to 1000 µL: every pipetful you add uses this volume)

A. Break the cells open

  1. Lysis buffer onto the pellet (4 mL) The pellet is taken up in about 4 mL. The buffer's 10 mM imidazole keeps weakly sticky bacterial proteins off the column later.
  2. Resuspend the pellet Pipet up and down until no lumps are left, so every cell meets the buffer.
  3. Keep a sample: whole cells (20 µL)
  4. Lysozyme (1 mg/mL in the tube, from the 10 mg/mL stock) Lysozyme digests the cell wall. Work out the volume, set the micropipette to it, then add it.
  5. 30 minutes on ice The bench clock runs on its own; "Speed up the wait" makes it run 60 times faster.
  6. Sonicate: six bursts of 10 s, 10 s on ice between them The probe shakes the liquid with ultrasound and breaks the softened cells open. Each burst warms the sample: watch its temperature on the sonicator's screen ("Sonicator screen" under the room shows it close up).
  7. Keep a sample: lysate

B. Clear the lysate

  1. Pour the lysate into the spin tube
  2. Make a balance tube Water in the balance tube, to the same volume as your lysate. A centrifuge spun unbalanced shakes itself apart, so it refuses to start.
  3. Both tubes into the rotor, opposite each other
  4. Close the lid, set the speed and the time, start The rotor's radius is 6.2 cm. The force is RCF = 1.118 × 10−5 × r (cm) × rpm2, in times the force of gravity (× g).
  5. Open the lid and take the tubes out
  6. Pour the cleared lysate off the pellet
  7. Keep samples: cleared lysate and pellet The pellet is taken up in lysis buffer to the lysate's volume before its sample is taken, so its lane can be compared.

C. The nickel column

  1. Swirl the resin, then 1 mL of slurry into the closed column The Ni-NTA beads settle in their bottle. 1 mL of 50% slurry gives a 0.5 mL bed, which holds about 4 mg of His-tagged protein.
  2. Equilibrate: 2.5 mL of lysis buffer, to the waste Five column volumes rinse the resin into the same buffer as the lysate. Never let the liquid fall below the top of the bed.
  3. Load the cleared lysate, collecting the flow-through Watch the top of the bed: the His-tagged protein binds to the nickel there.
  4. Wash twice with 4 mL of wash buffer 20 mM imidazole washes off bacterial proteins that stuck weakly; your protein stays bound.
  5. Elute: four fractions of 0.5 mL of elution buffer 250 mM imidazole competes the protein off the nickel. Set the micropipette to 500 µL and move the next tube under for each fraction.
