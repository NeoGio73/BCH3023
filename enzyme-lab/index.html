1At the bench

The enzyme cellobiase splits a colorless substrate into glucose and p-nitrophenol. You will run the reaction with the enzyme and without it, take a sample at each time, and stop each sample in a cuvette of stop solution, which also turns p-nitrophenol yellow. The more yellow, the more product.

Does the enzyme work faster when it is warmer? You will run the same reaction in an ice bath (0 °C), on the bench (22 °C) and in a warm bath (37 °C), stop each one after 2 minutes, and compare how much product was made.

Does the enzyme care about pH? You will run the same reaction at pH 5.0, pH 6.3 and pH 8.6, stop each one after 2 minutes, and compare how much product was made. This activity uses 3.0 mM substrate, because the pH buffer already in each tube dilutes it.

Does more enzyme make the reaction faster, and does it make more product in the end? You will run the same amount of substrate with a high and a low concentration of enzyme, and sample each at 1, 2 and 8 minutes.

Does more substrate make the reaction faster, and does it make more product in the end? You will run the same amount of enzyme with a high and a low concentration of substrate, and sample each at 1, 2 and 8 minutes.

2Standard curve

The graph plots your absorbance readings of the standards from Table 3 and draws the best straight line through them. To find how much p-nitrophenol is in a cuvette, find its absorbance on the upright axis, go across to the line, then down to the amount. Move the pointer over the graph, or use the slider, to read the line. The same curve serves every activity.

3Reaction rate

Activity 1: enzyme or no enzyme

The graph plots the amount of product in each cuvette against the time its sample was taken, using Table 4 (or Table 2 for any cuvette you have not finished in Table 4). Where the points rise in a straight line, the slope of that line is the initial rate of the reaction: the change in nmol divided by the change in minutes.

Activity 2: temperature

Each reaction was sampled once, at 2 minutes. Take the amount of p-nitrophenol at 0 minutes as 0 nmol, so the initial rate is the amount in the cuvette divided by 2 minutes. Use the amounts from Table 7 (or Table 6 if you have not finished Table 7). The graph plots your three rates against temperature.

Activity 3: pH

Each reaction was sampled once, at 2 minutes. Take the amount of p-nitrophenol at 0 minutes as 0 nmol, so the initial rate is the amount in the cuvette divided by 2 minutes. Use the amounts from Table 10 (or Table 9 if you have not finished Table 10). The graph plots your three rates against pH.

Activity 4: enzyme concentration

The graph plots the amount of product in each cuvette against the time its sample was taken, for the high and the low enzyme concentration, using Table 13 (or Table 12 for any cuvette you have not finished there). For each reaction, take the amount of product at 0 minutes as 0 nmol, so the initial rate is the amount of product at 1 minute divided by 1 minute.

Activity 5: substrate concentration

The graph plots the amount of product in each cuvette against the time its sample was taken, for the high and the low substrate concentration, using Table 16 (or Table 15 for any cuvette you have not finished there). For each reaction, take the amount of product at 0 minutes as 0 nmol, so the initial rate is the amount of product at 1 minute divided by 1 minute.
